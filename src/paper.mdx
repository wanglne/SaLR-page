<section id="overview" aria-labelledby="overview-title">

<h2 id="overview-title">Overview</h2>

<PaperFigure name="intro" alt="SaLR overview: latent reasoning baselines show higher jailbreak attack success and weaker separation of safe and unsafe representations; compact safety supervision improves the safety–utility balance." />

### Why Does Latent Space Reasoning Need Dedicated Safety Alignment?

Textual chain-of-thought (CoT) provides explicit intermediate steps that can be supervised directly, whereas latent reasoning does not expose a readable reasoning trace. Training on final responses therefore only indirectly constrains the preceding latent states. This motivates safety supervision at the transition from latent reasoning to response generation, as implemented in SaLR.

</section>

<section id="methodology" aria-labelledby="methodology-title">

<h2 id="methodology-title">Methodology</h2>

<PaperFigure name="method" alt="SaLR teacher–student training: compact safe-chains and mathematical reasoning traces supervise a latent student. Safety examples align a four-token response prefix, while math examples align a single position at answer onset." />

SaLR rewrites long form safety rationales into compact four-block safe-chains: request intent, risk assessment, response action, and safe follow-up. The action is selected from refuse, partial, or allow.

During training, the teacher learns from explicit reasoning traces and target responses, while the student reasons through latent states. SaLR aligns teacher and student hidden states across the first four response prefix positions (K = 4) for safety examples, and uses single position alignment at the answer onset for mathematical examples.

</section>

<section id="results" aria-labelledby="results-title">

<h2 id="results-title">Results</h2>

<Results />

Across 24 settings (3 model scales × 2 attacks × 4 benchmarks), SaLR achieves a mean jailbreak ASR of at most **3.1% in each setting**. On the 3B model, it achieves **59.23%** average math accuracy, close to CODI's **59.86%**, while using approximately **40× fewer generated tokens** than SFT-CoT + SafeChain. Its benign over-refusal rates are **2.0% on XSTest and 0.33% on OKTest.** Together, these results demonstrate a favorable balance between jailbreak robustness, reasoning utility, and benign request handling.

### Robustness to Attacks on the Reasoning Process

Beyond standard jailbreaks, we evaluate OverThink token inflation, H-CoT reasoning hijacking, Crescendo multi-turn attacks, and white-box perturbations of latent reasoning states.
